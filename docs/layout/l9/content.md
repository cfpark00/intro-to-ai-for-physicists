## Phenomenon hooks
- **The data is generated by the policy.** This single fact creates almost all of RL's special problems.
- **Credit assignment** as a phenomenon — how does the learner figure out which past action caused a current reward?
- **Sparse reward** — what happens when feedback is rare.
- **Exploration vs exploitation** — the master tension.
- **Reward hacking** — what models do when the reward signal is misspecified.

## Topics

### Why RL is different (the framing)
- Supervised learning: fixed dataset, fixed loss, gradient on labeled data. Distribution is *given*.
- RL: the agent's behavior *creates* the data. Distribution shifts as the policy changes.
- "Off-policy" issues — when data was generated by a different policy than the one being optimized.
- And: "in some sense quite similar" — viewing RL as iterated supervised learning with weighted updates.

### Minimal RL machinery
- **MDPs** — states, actions, rewards, transitions. Just enough notation.
- **Policy gradient** — REINFORCE, actor-critic.
- **PPO** — practical workhorse (also used in L6 RLHF, but here treated as real RL).
- **DPO** — was post-training in L6, but useful to contrast.
- **On-policy vs off-policy** — what the distinction actually means and why it matters.

### Hard problems (the meat of the lecture)
- **Credit assignment problem** — sparse, delayed feedback. The temporal-difference solution and its limits.
- **Sparse reward problem** — what happens when you can't bootstrap from frequent signal. Connection to exploration.
- **Exploration vs exploitation** — UCB, Thompson sampling, intrinsic-motivation methods.
- **Long-horizon problem** — temporal-difference bootstrapping breaks down. Hierarchical approaches.
- **Reward hacking** — agents that find exploits in the reward signal rather than the intended behavior.

### Modes of RL (lightly)
- **Offline RL** — fixed dataset, conservative updates.
- **Model-based RL** — learn a world model, plan in it. (Connects to L10.)
- **Multi-agent RL, self-play, Nash equilibria** — lightly mentioned, deeper in L12.
- **Evolutionary methods** — lightly mentioned, deeper in L12.

- Connection to the "open-endedness" themes in L12.
